# Resume Bullets — Merton–Altman Energy Credit Risk Engine

All bullets use only real results generated by this project (see `final_report.md`). Use the set matching the role you're targeting; do not mix numbers across sets without re-checking they're still accurate to your actual build.

## Finance (general)

- Built an end-to-end corporate credit-risk engine on 7 major Indian energy companies (FY2021–FY2025), implementing the Merton structural model and a modified Altman Z''-Score in Python, validating all 7 structural-model solves to convergence (residuals < 1e-8) and 28/28 behavioural sanity checks.
- Identified and quantified a persistent disagreement between market-based and accounting-based credit signals across 3 refiners and a diversified benchmark company, tracing the gap to a specific FY2023 margin-compression event and presenting it as a documented analytical finding rather than resolving it to a single score.

## Investment Banking

- Constructed a 7-company, 5-year comparable credit analysis (Debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, FCF/Debt, Altman Z'') for the Indian oil & gas sector, surfacing a robust #1/#7 peer ranking that held under 4 independent scoring-weight sensitivity tests.
- Designed and executed a multi-scenario stress-testing framework (profitability, debt, equity-market, and a combined bear case) showing a company's Distance-to-Default fell by up to 4.6 points under a correlated bear scenario versus under 1 point for any single isolated shock, informing a leverage-headroom assessment methodology.

## Equity Research

- Modelled market-implied Distance-to-Default for 7 listed Indian energy companies using the Merton (1974) option-pricing framework, solving a nonlinear 2-equation system via `scipy.optimize.root` and reconciling results against a 5-year Altman Z''-Score accounting trend to flag where market pricing was running ahead of trailing fundamentals.
- Ran a systematic sensitivity analysis across volatility, leverage, and market-value inputs, establishing that equity volatility — not debt or market-cap levels — was the single largest driver of model-implied credit risk across the entire peer set, a finding with direct implications for how forward-looking market data should be weighted in valuation work.

## Credit / Risk

- Implemented a full credit early-warning system across 8 leverage, liquidity, and market-based indicators for a 7-company peer set, correctly isolating a Red-level (4-indicator) warning for one company to a specific thin-margin fiscal year rather than a generic score.
- Built and validated a Merton structural credit model end-to-end (solver, distance-to-default, risk-neutral vs. physical default-probability distinction) and a documented, weight-tested composite credit score, while explicitly declining to build a supervised ML default classifier given a zero-default sample — and documenting the statistical reasoning for that decision.
